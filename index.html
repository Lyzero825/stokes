01 / 10 · What this is

Ampere is a page that keeps a count. Take an address as a boundary. Reconstruct a closed path of counterparties and transfers over a fixed window. Walk the path segment by segment. At every step, add the signed area element ½ (x dy − y dx) to a running total. That total is Γ. The sign of Γ says which way the loop has been winding. The magnitude says how hard.

The reading is the product. Nothing here forecasts. Nothing here asks you to trust a team’s character. An instrument does not ask for trust. It shows its work.

02 / 10 · The costume

For a few years now I have watched every wallet page repeat the one before it. A balance that pretends a boundary is a pile. A PnL chart that flattens a loop into a score. A gallery of pictures the address once touched, arranged as if contact were comprehension. The pitch never really changes. You open a tab, you watch numbers move, and you tell yourself you have read the wallet.

Something about that shape always bothered me. The thing being measured was almost never the thing that actually closed. The costume looked finished. The loop did not. There is nothing lasting in a score that forgets the path the moment the UI looks away.

03 / 10 · The sharper question

A wallet is not a pile. It is a boundary. Value crosses it, loops around it through counterparties, and sometimes returns changed. Most tools flatten that loop into a story and ask you to trust the flattening. Skip a transfer the UI did not like and the night did not happen.

So I started asking a different question. What would it mean to keep the circulation itself — the signed winding of a closed trade path — instead of inventing a narrative on top of it? No bolt between story and mechanism. One object, two languages: the sentence that says what is being kept, and the step that appends it.

Ampere is the answer I kept arriving at.

04 / 10 · On the name

André-Marie Ampère spent the early nineteenth century writing laws that treated current as something a closed path already knows. Circulating charge around a loop produces a field; the loop and the field answer to each other. Nothing in that statement asks you to believe a costume. It asks you to finish the integral around the wire.

That is the whole reason for the name. Not because a wallet is a circuit board, and not because markets are Maxwellian, but because the honest object is already a closed path — and the honest measurement is the circulation around it. Ampère did not invent the flow. He named a relationship that was always there. This page keeps the same kind of count for trade paths: signed, append-only, recomputable.

05 / 10 · Circulation, not costume

On the idea. Walk a closed curve. Sum the tangential pull along the edge. That sum is the circulation. It does not care how fast you walked. It cares which way the field wound, and how much area the winding enclosed in the signed sense. The rim and the sheet are the same fact written twice. You cannot decorate one side without answering to the other.

A wallet path works the same way. Plot counterparties and transfers as points in a plane the procedure fixes. Connect them in time order. Close the window — or fail to, which is itself a scar. Each edge contributes a signed triangle with the origin of the shoelace step. The running total is Γ. Most portfolio theater throws that total away and sells you a costume instead. Ampere keeps it.

On the economics. I wanted the reading to be honest by construction rather than by moderation. Most tokens are a story bolted onto a supply curve, and the bolt is where they break. Ampere has no bolt. The story — keep the running circulation of a closed wallet path — and the instrument — append each signed segment as it arrives — are the same object. If the answer is not derivable from the path and the step, it is not part of the instrument.

06 / 10 · Four shapes of a loop

A path is not a history dump. It is a closed sequence under a fixed window. In the illustrative desk the window is 256 steps — long enough to see a wind, short enough to recompute by hand. Longer windows are possible. They are not magic. They are more of the same integral.

Four shapes show up again and again. Independent drift keeps Γ near a living wind — the loop breathes without canceling itself. Wash loops spin hard and cancel; the costume looks busy, the integral refuses to be impressed. One-way drains refuse to close; Γ carries a scar that does not pretend to be symmetry. Whale ramps straighten the geometry until the wind looks polite and false — the exponent dies, the page still reports the number.

Ampere does not grade these shapes with adjectives. Open the instrument and scrub the regimes yourself. The integral is the grade.

08 / 10 · Why 2π

There is one constant in the whole procedure that matters for orientation. A full wind is a turn of 2π in the signed sense the instrument uses for display. It is the natural unit of a revolution, the same way a meter is a unit of length. Nobody on this desk picked it because it felt branded. The point of the constant is that nobody picked it.

When Γ / 2π crosses an integer, the path has wound once in that signed sense. Nothing on the page announces the crossing as a reward. Nothing in the procedure pays you for it. It simply happens, and the count moves. The winding was happening before the chart. It will outlast it.

source / of / code

The whole measurement collapses to one step. Paste it into a console and get the same number the desk prints. Nothing below claims to be the full chain-ingestion pipeline. It is the equality the rest of the page is built to protect — the product with its insides on the outside. 

// Ampere — circulation step (shoelace segment)
// Γ_{n+1} = Γ_n + ½ (x_n y_{n+1} − x_{n+1} y_n)

function stepGamma(G, x0, y0, x1, y1) {
  return G + 0.5 * (x0 * y1 - x1 * y0);
}

function circulation(pts) {
  let G = 0;
  for (let i = 0; i < pts.length - 1; i++) {
    const a = pts[i], b = pts[i + 1];
    G = stepGamma(G, a.x, a.y, b.x, b.y);
  }
  return G;
}

const TWO_PI = Math.PI * 2;
// winds ≈ Γ / TWO_PI — nobody chose 2π; it is orientation

From the drafter

I want to be plain about what this is and what it is not, because the honest version is more interesting than the hyped one. Ampere is a small idea on purpose. It is not a company with a roadmap suite. It is a desk that keeps a circulation count for closed wallet paths, and a manuscript that refuses to bolt a costume onto that count.

What is here now is enough to see the thought run and to argue with it: the teaching spine, the instrument, the step in source. The gauge shows sample telemetry and says so in plain text, because I would rather show you the true shape of the thing than dress up a network that is not live yet. If it is wrong, it should be easy to show that in a console. If there is something here, it should be easy to build the next thing on top of it — deeper, in the open.

I am not going to tell you this is inevitable or that it wins. I genuinely don’t know. What I believe is that wallets are boundaries, that boundaries deserve integrals rather than costumes, and that trust has to rest on something you can recompute. Beyond that, the future of an idea is not the author’s to assign. We set it down with nothing hidden and nothing promised, and the people who keep looking decide whether the field is worth staying in.

That is the honest version of the pitch. The paper is the map. Come help me build the territory.
